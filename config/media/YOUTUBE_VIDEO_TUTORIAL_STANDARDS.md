# Vision Loop — Tutorial Video Production Standards

> These standards apply to every tutorial video published under the Vision Loop brand — long-form episodes and YouTube Shorts.
> The goal: every video feels like a knowledgeable friend explaining something clearly, not a corporate lecture.

---

## 1. 🎯 The Core Principle

**Teach one thing. Teach it completely. Make it feel easy.**

Every video must answer a single specific question — e.g. *"How do I register for GST as a sole proprietor?"*. If the video tries to answer two questions, split it into two videos.

---

## 2. ⏱️ Structure — Every Long-Form Video Must Follow This Flow

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  HOOK          │  0:00 – 0:45  │  Why should I watch?   │
│  CONTEXT       │  0:45 – 2:00  │  What is this about?   │
│  CORE CONTENT  │  2:00 – 9:30  │  The actual teaching   │
│  RECAP         │  9:30 – 10:15 │  What did we cover?    │
│  CTA           │  10:15 – end  │  What to do next?      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2.1 The Hook (First 45 Seconds — NON-NEGOTIABLE)
- **Open with a problem or a surprising fact** — not with "Hi, welcome to Vision Loop."
- Hook formula: *"If you are doing [X], you are losing [Y]. Here is the fix."*
- Examples of good hooks:
  - *"Did you know you can get a GST number without a rent agreement? Most people don't."*
  - *"Filing your GST return late costs ₹50 a day. Most people don't know they're late."*
- The hook must make the viewer feel: **"This video is for me."**

### 2.2 Chapter Jump Points (Mandatory for Long-Form)
- Every long-form video must have **YouTube chapter timestamps** in the description.
- Minimum 5 chapters, recommended 8–10.
- Chapters must be self-descriptive — viewer should know what they'll learn just from reading the chapter list.
- Bad chapter name: *"Part 3"*
- Good chapter name: *"How to fill the Principal Place of Business tab"*

### 2.3 The Recap
- Spend 45 seconds summarising the 3–5 key takeaways.
- Spoken as bullet points — "So today you learned: first... second... third..."
- This is what the viewer will remember and share.

## 3. 🎥 Visual Style — What Goes On Screen

### 3.1 For Portal / Software Tutorials
- **Full-screen screen recording** of the actual website or app.
- Use a **red highlight circle** around every button or field before clicking it — give viewers 1–2 seconds to see it.
- Add **numbered step overlays** (e.g. Step 1, Step 2) in a corner badge.
- Zoom in when entering text in a field — the text must be clearly readable.
- Presenter face in **small Picture-in-Picture (PiP)** corner — bottom left or bottom right.

### 3.2 For Explanation / Concept Scenes
- Use **simple text-on-screen infographics** — white background, clean font, no clutter.
- Animate one bullet point at a time as it is spoken — not all at once.
- Use **two-column layouts** for comparisons (e.g. Benefits vs. Drawbacks).
- Use **coloured highlight boxes** for important numbers or warnings (red for penalties, green for benefits).

### 3.3 What NOT to Use
- ❌ No drone footage for non-travel content
- ❌ No stock footage of people shaking hands, typing on keyboards, or looking at graphs
- ❌ No slide-deck style presentations (PowerPoint-feel is boring)
- ❌ No cluttered graphics with 10+ elements on screen at once
- ❌ No animated 3D logos that play for more than 3 seconds

## 4. 🎙️ Voiceover & Presenter Tone

### 4.1 Language
- **Primary:** Hindi (`hi-IN`) — conversational, not formal.
- **Technical terms** (form names, legal section numbers, portal button names) spoken in **English** — do not translate `"TRN"` or `"GSTIN"` or `"Form GST REG-01"`.
- Speak as if explaining to a **smart friend who is not a tax expert** — not to a CA professional.

### 4.2 Tone Rules
- Use **"आप"** not **"आपको inform किया जाता है"** — friendly, not bureaucratic.
- Use **short sentences**. If a sentence needs a comma, split it into two sentences.
- **Pause after every key instruction** — give the viewer time to absorb it.
- It is okay to say *"यह थोड़ा confusing लग सकता है — लेकिन घबराएं नहीं, हम step by step करेंगे."*
- **Never read text verbatim from the screen** — explain it in your own words.

### 4.3 Pacing
- Normal explanation scenes: **relaxed pace** (~130 words/minute).
- Portal live demos: **deliberately slow** — speak slower than feels natural. Viewers need time to follow along.
- Warnings / liability scenes: **even slower** — these are the most important moments.

### 4.4 Audio Quality Standards
- Target loudness: **-14.0 LUFS** (YouTube standard)
- Sample rate: 48,000 Hz
- No background noise, no echo
- Do not record in a room with hard walls — use a soft furnishing room or hang cloth/blankets

---

## 5. ✂️ Editing Rules

### 5.1 Cuts & Pacing
- **Never leave dead air** — if the presenter pauses for more than 1.5 seconds, cut it.
- Use **jump cuts** freely — they are preferred over slow fades between talking-head segments.
- Use **zoom cuts** (push in on the speaker) to re-engage attention at key moments.
- Every new chapter / scene should start with a **chapter title card** (2–3 seconds) showing the chapter number and name.

### 5.2 On-Screen Text Overlays
- **Key numbers, deadlines, and form names must always appear as on-screen text** — not just spoken.
  - Example: When saying *"GSTR-1 is due by the 11th"* — show `GSTR-1 → Due: 11th of Every Month` on screen simultaneously.
- Overlays must stay on screen for at least **3 seconds** — enough time to read.
- Use a **consistent font** across all videos — one serif for headings, one sans-serif for body text.

### 5.3 Transitions
- Use **simple cuts** for most transitions — no wipes, spins, or zoom-blurs.
- Use a **brief fade-to-black** only between major sections (e.g. between Context and Core Content).
- Chapter title cards act as natural transition moments.

### 5.4 Background Music
- Use **subtle ambient/lo-fi instrumental music** at -20dB under the voiceover — just enough to fill silence.
- Music must **fade to zero** during any warning/liability scene — these moments must feel serious.
- Music must be **copyright-free** or licensed (YouTube Audio Library preferred).

## 6. 🔒 Non-Negotiable Compliance Rules

### 6.1 PII Blurring (Every Video)
Every video showing a real portal demo must apply **25px Gaussian Blur** over:
- PAN numbers (show format only: `XXXXX9999X`)
- Aadhaar numbers (show last 4 only: `XXXX-XXXX-4390`)
- Bank account numbers & IFSC codes
- OTP values during demonstrations
- Full residential addresses

### 6.2 AI Disclosure (Every Video)
A visible disclaimer must appear on-screen at the start and in the description:
> *"This content was produced with AI assistance and is for educational purposes only. Please consult a licensed Chartered Accountant (CA) before taking financial or tax decisions."*

### 6.3 Content Rating
- All Vision Loop tutorials must be suitable for a **Universal (U) / All Ages** audience.
- No political commentary, no religion, no aggressive language.

### 6.4 Grievance Officer
- Every video description must include: `Grievance Officer: visionloop.in@gmail.com`

## 8. 🖼️ Thumbnail Standards

A thumbnail must communicate the video's value in **under 2 seconds of glancing**.

### Rules:
- **One clear visual + one short text line** — maximum.
- Text must be **large enough to read on a mobile screen** (minimum 60pt equivalent).
- Use **high contrast** — light text on dark background or vice versa.
- Avoid cluttered thumbnails with 5+ elements.
- Include a **genuine human face with an expression** (curiosity, surprise, or pointing) — this significantly improves click-through rate.
- Do NOT use clickbait images that have nothing to do with the video content.
- Thumbnail text should match the promise of the title — never be misleading.

### Good thumbnail example for GST Registration video:
- Background: dark blue gradient
- Left: presenter pointing at a GST REG-06 certificate
- Right: large text — `"GST बिना Rent Agreement के? ✅"`
- Bottom right: Vision Loop logo bug

## 9. 🚫 What Makes a Bad Tutorial (Things to Actively Avoid)

| Bad Practice | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Long intro with logo animation | Viewers drop off in the first 15 seconds |
| Reading from a script robotically | Sounds unnatural — loses trust |
| Covering too many topics in one video | Viewer gets confused about what they learned |
| Skipping the "why" — just showing steps | Viewers don't know when to apply the knowledge |
| No chapter timestamps | Viewers can't navigate; watch time drops |
| Showing liabilities at the end only | Most viewers leave before the end — put important warnings early |
| Using jargon without explaining it | Creates anxiety, not understanding |
| Not showing the actual portal / screen | Viewers can't follow along at home |
| Drone shots in a software tutorial | Irrelevant visuals break trust and look amateurish |

## 10. ✅ Pre-Publish Checklist

Before publishing any Vision Loop tutorial, verify:

- [ ] Video opens with a hook (problem/surprising fact) — NOT a greeting
- [ ] Topic is singular and clearly stated within first 30 seconds
- [ ] YouTube chapter timestamps added to description (min. 5 chapters)
- [ ] All portal steps are numbered and annotated with red highlight circles
- [ ] Key numbers and deadlines shown as on-screen text overlays
- [ ] Liabilities / risks / warnings section included — not omitted
- [ ] PAN, Aadhaar, OTP values blurred on-screen
- [ ] AI disclosure present in first 30 seconds and in description
- [ ] Audio loudness target: -14.0 LUFS
- [ ] Closed captions available (Hindi primary, English secondary)
- [ ] Content rating: Universal (U)
- [ ] Grievance Officer email in description
- [ ] Thumbnail is clear, high-contrast, mobile-readable
- [ ] Background music is copyright-free
- [ ] Recap of 3–5 key takeaways at the end
- [ ] Clear CTA (subscribe / service link / free download) at the end
